Welcome! I am a quantitative health services researcher and PhD candidate in Health Services and Policy Research at Boston University School of Public Health. My research goal is to better understand how clinicians respond to payment and delivery reforms, such as selective contracting and telemedicine. My dissertation project focuses on using low-value services measures to measure the value of physician practices for value-based networks. I use econometric techniques and measurement science to large datasets, including Medicare fee-for-service claims and Veterans administrative data, electronic health records, and survey. Before pursuing PhD training, I worked in government management in global health and practiced clinical internal medicine. I received my MPH in health policy and management, BS in nursing, and BA in philosophy in South Korea.

Association between Telehealth and Patient Experience in Veterans Affairs Primary Care, 2018–2021. <em>Under review.</em> <details class="abstract"><summary>[abstract]</summary><div class="abstract-body">Background: Telehealth accounts for a growing share of primary care delivery in the Veterans Health Administration, yet evidence on patient experience using policy-aligned measures and accounting for patient selection remains limited.<br><br> Objective: To examine the association between video visit use and patient experience in primary care across 13 patient experience measures. <br><br> Design: Cross-sectional study using VHA patient experience surveys linked to administrative data from October 2018 to September 2021. To account for patient selection into video visits, we used an instrumental variable approach leveraging broadband availability. Multiple hypothesis testing across 13 outcomes was addressed using Bonferroni correction. <br><br>Participants: 676,772 respondents with a primary care index visit
Main Measures: Exposure is a binary indicator of having at least one video visit. Outcomes are thirteen patient experience measures across five domains: overall ratings, timely access to care, communication with clinicians, care coordination, and comprehensive primary care. Outcomes were dichotomized as top-box indicators. <br><br>Key Results: Video visit use was not associated with statistically significant differences in 12 of 13 patient experience measures compared with in-person and telephone care. Video visit use was associated with a lower likelihood of being asked about depressive symptoms (adjusted difference, ‐0.54; 95% CI, ‐0.87 to ‐0.21; Bonferroni-adjusted P= .02). <br><br>Conclusions: Video visits were not associated with worse patient experience across most domains. However, screening for depressive symptoms may occur less frequently during video encounters, suggesting a potential quality gap that could be addressed through targeted screening protocols for video visits.</div></details>
<p><strong>Song S</strong>, Auty SG, Yee CA, Chalmers K, Pizer SD. Prospective Low-Value Service Composite to Predict Spending and Quality. <em>In preparation.</em> <details class="abstract"><summary>[abstract]</summary><div class="abstract-body"><strong>Objective:</strong> To develop a practice-level low-value service (LVS) composite for predicting orthopedic spending.<br><br><strong>Study Setting and Design:</strong> Cross-sectional study of orthopedic practices serving Medicare beneficiaries enrolled in the Veterans Health Administration in 2019. We used a three-step approach: (1) estimating risk-adjusted practice-level rates for five candidate orthopedic LVS measures using hierarchical logistic models; (2) selecting measures predicting higher orthopedic spending without evidence of lower readmission; and (3) constructing volume-weighted composites of standardized rates and comparing their performance with an all-5-LVS composite and Merit-based Incentive Payment System (MIPS) benchmark scores within Hospital Referral Regions.<br><br><strong>Data Sources and Analytic Sample:</strong> Medicare Fee-for-Service claims (2017–2020) linked to the Medicare Data on Provider Practice and Specialty file, Master Beneficiary Summary File, and Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) MIPS performance file. The sample included 461,874 Medicare-enrolled veterans with at least one orthopedist visit in 2019, attributed to 5,113 orthopedic practices identified by Tax Identification Number.<br><br><strong>Principal Findings:</strong> Advanced back imaging and spinal injection met the prespecified selection criteria. A 1-SD increase in the resulting 2-LVS composite predicted $393.12 higher orthopedic spending (p &lt; .01). Its prediction of 30-day readmission was positive but not statistically significant (0.35 percentage-point increase; 95% CI, −0.50 to 1.20). Neither the all-5-LVS composite nor the MIPS overall score predicted either outcome.<br><br><strong>Conclusion:</strong> Outcome-predictive LVS composites may provide a useful approach for measuring orthopedic practice spending performance. However, composite performance was sensitive to specification choices, so policy applications may require careful alignment between measurement design and policy objectives. Outcome-predictive selection criteria may help guide these decisions.</div></details></p>
