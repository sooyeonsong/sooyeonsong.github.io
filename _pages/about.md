I am a PhD candidate in Health Services and Policy Research (Health Economics) at Boston University School of Public Health, and a Graduate Fellow at the Partnered Evidence-based Policy Resource Center (PEPReC) at the VA Boston Healthcare System. My research examines how clinicians respond to payment and delivery reforms such as selective contracting and telemedicine. My dissertation measures the quality and value of physician practices to improve selective contracting.

Before my doctoral training, I worked in government management in global health at the Korea International Cooperation Agency (KOICA) and the World Health Organization, and practiced as a registered nurse in internal medicine. I received my MPH in Health Care Management and Policy, BS in Nursing, and BA in Philosophy from Seoul National University.

You can find my CV [here](/files/Song_CV.pdf).

Telehealth and the work behavior of mental health clinicians. <em>Health Affairs Scholar.</em> 2026;4(4). <details class="abstract"><summary>[Abstract]</summary><div class="abstract-body"><strong>Introduction.</strong> Telehealth delivers more than half of mental health care in the United States amid persistent mental health workforce shortages. However, little is known about how telehealth relates to clinicians' work behavior, specifically their visits per clinic day and probability of turnover.<br><br><strong>Methods.</strong> We examined mental health clinicians working at the Veterans Health Administration who collectively delivered 26 million visits. We regressed clinicians' monthly visit volume (supply), number of clinic days worked (input), visits per clinic day (throughput), and probability of turnover (retention) on the proportion of their mental health visits delivered via video. We used variation in broadband availability as an instrumental variable for video visit use.<br><br><strong>Results.</strong> A 10 percentage-point higher proportion of video visits was associated with no significant difference in total visits, 0.9 fewer clinic days per month (8.5% of the sample mean), 0.5 more visits per clinic day (6.2% of the sample mean), and a 0.4 percentage-point lower probability of monthly turnover (46% of the sample mean). Among psychologists and social workers, higher video share was associated with more visits per clinic day and fewer clinic days, whereas psychiatrists showed fewer visits per clinic day.<br><br><strong>Conclusion.</strong> Telehealth may shape clinicians' work behavior, including visits per clinic day, allocation of clinic days, and retention.</div></details> <a class="pub-link" href="https://academic.oup.com/healthaffairsscholar/article/4/4/qxag070/8540003" target="_blank" rel="noopener">[Full paper]</a></p>

<p>2. <strong>Song S</strong>, Jang T, Lum J, Nguyen P, Pizer SD, Yee CA. Association between Telehealth and Patient Experience in Veterans Affairs Primary Care, 2018&ndash;2021. <em>Under review.</em> <details class="abstract"><summary>[Abstract]</summary><div class="abstract-body"><strong>Background.</strong> Telehealth accounts for a growing share of primary care delivery in the Veterans Health Administration, yet evidence on patient experience using policy-aligned measures and accounting for patient selection remains limited.<br><br><strong>Objective.</strong> To examine the association between video visit use and patient experience in primary care across 13 patient experience measures.<br><br><strong>Design.</strong> Cross-sectional study using VHA patient experience surveys linked to administrative data from October 2018 to September 2021. To account for patient selection into video visits, we used an instrumental variable approach leveraging broadband availability. Multiple hypothesis testing across 13 outcomes was addressed using Bonferroni correction.<br><br><strong>Participants.</strong> 676,772 respondents with a primary care index visit. Main Measures: Exposure is a binary indicator of having at least one video visit. Outcomes are thirteen patient experience measures across five domains: overall ratings, timely access to care, communication with clinicians, care coordination, and comprehensive primary care. Outcomes were dichotomized as top-box indicators.<br><br><strong>Key Results.</strong> Video visit use was not associated with statistically significant differences in 12 of 13 patient experience measures compared with in-person and telephone care. Video visit use was associated with a lower likelihood of being asked about depressive symptoms (adjusted difference, &minus;0.54; 95% CI, &minus;0.87 to &minus;0.21; Bonferroni-adjusted P = .02).<br><br><strong>Conclusions.</strong> Video visits were not associated with worse patient experience across most domains. However, screening for depressive symptoms may occur less frequently during video encounters, suggesting a potential quality gap that could be addressed through targeted screening protocols for video visits.</div></details></p>

<p>3. <strong>Song S</strong>, Auty SG, Yee CA, Chalmers K, Pizer SD. Prospective Low-Value Service Composite to Predict Spending and Quality. <em>In preparation.</em> <details class="abstract"><summary>[Abstract]</summary><div class="abstract-body"><strong>Objective.</strong> To develop a practice-level low-value service (LVS) composite for predicting orthopedic spending.<br><br><strong>Study Setting and Design.</strong> Cross-sectional study of orthopedic practices serving Medicare beneficiaries enrolled in the Veterans Health Administration in 2019. We used a three-step approach: (1) estimating risk-adjusted practice-level rates for five candidate orthopedic LVS measures using hierarchical logistic models; (2) selecting measures predicting higher orthopedic spending without evidence of lower readmission; and (3) constructing volume-weighted composites of standardized rates and comparing their performance with an all-5-LVS composite and Merit-based Incentive Payment System (MIPS) benchmark scores within Hospital Referral Regions.<br><br><strong>Data Sources and Analytic Sample.</strong> Medicare Fee-for-Service claims (2017&ndash;2020) linked to the Medicare Data on Provider Practice and Specialty file, Master Beneficiary Summary File, and Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) MIPS performance file. The sample included 461,874 Medicare-enrolled veterans with at least one orthopedist visit in 2019, attributed to 5,113 orthopedic practices identified by Tax Identification Number.<br><br><strong>Principal Findings.</strong> Advanced back imaging and spinal injection met the prespecified selection criteria. A 1-SD increase in the resulting 2-LVS composite predicted $393.12 higher orthopedic spending (p &lt; .01). Its prediction of 30-day readmission was positive but not statistically significant (0.35 percentage-point increase; 95% CI, &minus;0.50 to 1.20). Neither the all-5-LVS composite nor the MIPS overall score predicted either outcome.<br><br><strong>Conclusion.</strong> Outcome-predictive LVS composites may provide a useful approach for measuring orthopedic practice spending performance. However, composite performance was sensitive to specification choices, so policy applications may require careful alignment between measurement design and policy objectives. Outcome-predictive selection criteria may help guide these decisions.</div></details></p>
